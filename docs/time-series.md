# Time-series properties of prices and returns

The time-series object is the market claim. Once aggregate consumption and market dividends are calibrated, the Euler equation must accommodate the equity premium, the risk-free rate, return volatility, and market $$\log(P/D)$$. That is the usual test of a consumption-based model. It is not the test that resolves the value premium.

In Table VII the market row is that object. Printed expected return 8.56 percent against 7.53 in the model; mean $$\log(P/D)$$ 3.34 against 3.24. The same investor also produces a risk-free rate of 1.58 percent against 0.91 in the data.

Melin and Zhang (2026) keep that object and put climate into consumption. Their twenty-percent rise in the *market* premium at $$3^{\circ}$$C is still a time-series statement. It does not rank firms.
